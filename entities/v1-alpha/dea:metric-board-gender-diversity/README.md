## 2. Definition

Within Sustainability & ESG, Board Gender Diversity (BGD) is used to gauge conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. It is defined as Women on Board / Total Board Members; the result is stated in percent (%). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The share of board seats held by women - the governance metric that moved from aspiration to statute in several major markets, some of which now mandate floors or explain-why-not disclosure, and institutional investors coordinate their votes against laggard boards. Its strategic significance is partly signal, partly mechanism: board composition correlates with different risk oversight behavior in the governance research, and director pipelines genuinely broaden when boards change - but the metric is also the most visible single number in the entire ESG apparatus, which makes it a lightning rod for both advocacy and backlash. Read with independence and skill-mix data: a diverse board that is uniformly non-independent is composition theater, and the honest governance question is whether diversity arrives with genuine mandate.

## Formula

```
Women on Board / Total Board Members
```
