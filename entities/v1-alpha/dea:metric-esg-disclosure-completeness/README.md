## 2. Definition

ESG Disclosure Completeness (ESGDC) measures conformance to standards, plans, or obligations in the Investor Relations & Capital Markets domain. It is defined as Disclosed ESG Metrics per Framework / Required Metrics; the result is stated in percent (%). In Listed companies, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

The share of required or expected environmental, social, and governance disclosures actually published - the reporting-infrastructure metric whose strategic significance has migrated rapidly: sustainability disclosure regimes (the CSRD/ESRS framework in Europe, and climate-specific mandates elsewhere) have converted ESG reporting from voluntary narrative into a compliance regime with assurance requirements, and completeness against the framework is the primary evidence of readiness. The metric's diagnostic value is the gap map: which disclosure topics are missing identifies precisely where the underlying data does not yet exist - because disclosure completeness is downstream of data infrastructure, and a missing disclosure is usually a missing measurement system, not a missing sentence. Read with the assurance-readiness dimension: publishing disclosures that cannot survive limited assurance is completeness without credibility, and the transition to assured reporting is where incomplete-but-honest and complete-but-unverifiable diverge in consequence.

## Formula

```
Disclosed ESG Metrics per Framework / Required Metrics
```
