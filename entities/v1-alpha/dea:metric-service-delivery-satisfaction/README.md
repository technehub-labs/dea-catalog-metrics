## 2. Definition

Service Delivery Satisfaction (SDS) addresses the engagement or satisfaction of the people involved and is a fixture of public sector & government reporting. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Its calculation is Satisfied Citizen Survey Responses / Responses, measured in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. It originates from and is most used in Public sector practice.

## 3. Context

Citizen-reported satisfaction with public services - the outcome-side metric of government delivery, and the corrective to the input-and-output bias of traditional public administration measurement: budgets and throughput measure what government does, satisfaction measures whether citizens experienced it as service. Its strategic significance is legitimacy economics: satisfaction with services correlates with institutional trust, and trust is the asset that funds compliance (tax morale), cooperation, and the political capital reforms require - which is why modern public-management frameworks (the SERVQUAL-informed tradition in citizen-service measurement) insist on measuring experience rather than assuming it. The honest design is service-specific and comparable over time, since whole-of-government satisfaction indices move slowly and carry little actionable content. Read with the equity dimension: satisfaction averaged across the population can conceal systematically worse experience for the citizens who depend on the service most - the pattern that satisfaction means without equity analysis never surfaces.

## Formula

```
Satisfied Citizen Survey Responses / Responses
```
