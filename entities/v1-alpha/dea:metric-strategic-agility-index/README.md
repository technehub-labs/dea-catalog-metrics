## 2. Definition

Within Strategy & Portfolio, Strategic Agility Index (SAI) is used to gauge capability maturity or development. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. The formula is Weighted Index of Decision Cycle Time and Reallocation Speed - reported in index (0-100 or normalized). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

A composite assessment of how fast the organization senses change and reconfigures in response - typically combining sensing coverage, decision latency, resource reallocation speed, and the observed success rate of pivots. Its strategic weight is regime change: in stable markets, efficiency wins; in volatile ones, the ability to redeploy people and capital faster than competitors becomes the primary advantage, which is what this index is constructed to measure. The empirical anchor comes from corporate-finance research showing most large firms reallocate capital slower than their environments change, which makes reallocation speed the load-bearing component. The index carries weight only when grounded in observed behavior - time from signal to decision in past episodes - rather than survey self-assessment, since organizations rate their own agility generously until a real event times them.
