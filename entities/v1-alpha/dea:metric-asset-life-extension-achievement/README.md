## 2. Definition

Asset Life Extension Achievement (ALEA) measures the economic value created or preserved in the Energy Transition & Renewables domain. It is a headline indicator: movement in it prompts leadership questions about strategy and resourcing. It is computed as Turbines Operating beyond Design Life / Turbines at Design Life, expressed in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Its use is concentrated in Renewable energy.

## 3. Context

Asset life extension achievement measures progress in extending operating life beyond original design specifications, and it is the capital-deferral metric of energy-transition economics: extending a wind farm or plant 5-10 years beyond design life defers decommissioning and replacement capital, and because the asset is fully capitalized, extended-years production is nearly margin-pure. The engineering-honesty requirement is condition-based: life extension is only legitimate when component condition (foundations, blades, gearboxes, structural steel) is measured against remaining-life requirements, and regulators increasingly require engineering justification rather than default continuation. The trade-off is efficiency: extended-life assets typically operate at lower performance (degradation, outdated technology) than replacement would deliver. Read with condition-assessment rigor, degradation trajectory (is extension economical at the degraded output?), and safety-critical-component replacement history.

## Formula

```
Turbines Operating beyond Design Life / Turbines at Design Life
```
