## 2. Definition

Faculty Publication Rate (FPR) captures growth over time for organizations tracking education. It is defined as Peer-Reviewed Publications per Faculty FTE; the result is stated in ratio (x:1 or x). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. In Education, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Publications per faculty member, conventionally normalized by field - the productivity metric of the academic research enterprise, and the one that promotion systems worldwide actually reward, which makes it simultaneously the driver of scholarly output and the generator of the field's best-known distortion: metric pressure selects for publishable quantity in ways that can crowd depth. Its strategic significance is reputational compounding: publication record drives hiring, ranking, and funding review, and the institutional aggregate is the input to the ranking scores that universities compete on. The honest use is field-normalized and quality-weighted - citation impact, venue tier - since raw counts across disciplines are meaningless, and within-field counts reward the minimum publishable unit over the substantial contribution.

Peer-Reviewed Publications per Faculty FTE
