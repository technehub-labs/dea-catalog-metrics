## 2. Definition

A Mining & Metals indicator, Energy Intensity of Extraction (EIE) quantifies environmental and social sustainability performance. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. The formula is Energy Consumed / Ore Processed - reported in ratio (x:1 or x). It should be driven down, since lower readings signal healthier performance. It is a standard measure in the Mining & metals sector.

## 3. Context

Energy intensity of extraction is energy consumed per tonne of ore or unit of metal, and it is where a mine's exposure to power prices, grid decarbonization, and automation trade-offs becomes visible. Deepening pits and declining grades mechanically raise intensity, so a flat number may actually signal efficiency gains being cancelled by geology. It also feeds Scope 2 emissions: a tonne moved by diesel carries a different carbon and cost profile than one moved by trolley-assist electric haulage. Read with energy cost per tonne, emissions intensity, and renewable supply share to see whether the intensity trend is structural or fixable.
