## 2. Definition

Hazardous Waste Generation (HWG) captures environmental and social sustainability performance for organizations tracking sustainability & esg. The formula is Hazardous Waste Produced per Period - reported in tonnes. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. Lower values are better; each reduction is an improvement. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The tonnage of waste classified hazardous under applicable regulation that the organization produces - the compliance-anchored waste metric, where the classification itself is the strategic burden: hazardous status determines handling, transport, treatment, and disposal cost, and a waste stream reclassified as hazardous changes the operation without changing the chemistry. Strategic significance is lifecycle exposure: hazardous waste carries cradle-to-grave liability that outlives the facility that generated it, which makes this metric a risk ledger as much as a throughput measure. The productive read is normalized and trended - hazardous waste per unit of product falling indicates process substitution at the source, the response regulators prefer, while rising disposal cost per tonne usually indicates the treatment market tightening rather than the operation worsening.
