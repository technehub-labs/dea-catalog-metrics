## 2. Definition

A Nonprofit & NGO indicator, Program Outcome Attainment (POA) quantifies how effectively a process or entity achieves its intended outcome. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Its calculation is Programs Meeting Outcome Targets / Programs Delivered, measured in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. It originates from and is most used in Nonprofit & NGO practice.

## 3. Context

Program outcome attainment measures the share of program goals achieved against defined outcome targets - the impact metric that the entire sector has been pushed toward by results-based funding, and the honest counterweight to activity metrics like reach. Its measurement difficulty is attribution: outcomes have multiple causes (a community's health improved - how much was the program?), so rigorous attainment requires comparison groups, baseline measurement, or at minimum pre-post trend with stated attribution assumptions. Target-setting honesty is the gaming vector: soft targets guarantee attainment. Read with the methodology behind attribution claims, outcome-per-dollar efficiency, and beneficiary-level variation (average attainment can hide distributional failure where the easiest-to-serve are reached and the hardest are quietly dropped).

## Formula

```
Programs Meeting Outcome Targets / Programs Delivered
```
