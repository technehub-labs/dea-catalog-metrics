## 2. Definition

Carbon Intensity per Revenue (CIR-S) captures environmental and social sustainability performance for organizations tracking sustainability & esg. Its calculation is Total CO2e Emissions / Revenue, measured in ratio (x:1 or x). It is applied across virtually every sector. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

Emissions normalized by output - conventionally tonnes of CO2-equivalent per unit of revenue, or per physical unit of production where the operation is a commodity - the efficiency form of carbon accounting that the intensity-versus-absolute distinction lives on. Its strategic role is decoupling evidence: an organization whose absolute emissions rise while intensity falls is growing dirtier-but-efficiently, and whether that counts as progress is the central honesty question of corporate climate reporting - intensity metrics flatter growth, which is why disclosure regimes increasingly require absolute numbers alongside. The normalization choice is the whole game: per revenue, per tonne of product, per passenger-kilometer, and per unit of energy each tell different stories for the same company, and intensity improvements can arrive from accounting (divestitures, outsourcing) with no operational change at all.

## Formula

```
Total CO2e Emissions / Revenue
```
