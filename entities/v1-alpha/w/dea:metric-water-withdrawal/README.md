## 2. Definition

Water Withdrawal (WW) measures environmental and social sustainability performance in the Sustainability & ESG domain. It is computed as Total Water Withdrawn per Period, expressed in kilolitres (kl). It is applied across virtually every sector. It is a headline indicator: movement in it prompts leadership questions about strategy and resourcing. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

The total volume of water taken from all sources - surface, ground, municipal, and produced - the withdrawal-side gauge of water stewardship that the CDP questionnaire and emerging disclosure regimes standardized. Its strategic weight is geography: water risk is intensely local, and a company whose withdrawals sit in water-stressed basins faces regulatory, community, and physical risk that identical volumes in water-rich regions do not - so the total is a starting point and the stressed-basin share is the metric that carries risk information. The distinction from consumption matters: most withdrawn water is returned (as effluent), and the consumptive-use share is what the basin actually loses. Read with discharge quality: withdrawals that return polluted water concentrate their impact downstream, which is why stewardship metrics track both sides of the pipe.
