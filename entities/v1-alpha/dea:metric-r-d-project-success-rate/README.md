## 2. Definition

R&D Project Success Rate (RPSR2) measures how effectively a process or entity achieves its intended outcome in the R&D and Innovation domain. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Projects Meeting Objectives / Projects Started; the result is stated in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

The share of development projects that hit their technical and commercial criteria - the conversion metric of the R&D portfolio, and the honesty check on pipeline enthusiasm: organizations that never measure it cannot distinguish a portfolio that produces assets from one that produces activity. Its strategic weight is learning economics: each project termination decision is information about selection criteria, and organizations with a measured success rate get better at killing projects early, which is the single largest lever on R&D productivity. The definitional honesty is the criteria: a success defined loosely enough (any revenue, any publication) will be met and teach nothing, and the disciplined version pins technical success, schedule, and commercial threshold at approval. Read with the phase mix: front-end exploration projects should fail often - a uniformly high success rate indicates the portfolio is only doing low-risk work.

## Formula

```
Projects Meeting Objectives / Projects Started
```
