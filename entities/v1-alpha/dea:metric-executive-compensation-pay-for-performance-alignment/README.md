## 2. Definition

Within Corporate Governance, Executive Compensation Pay-for-Performance Alignment (ECPA) is used to gauge the economic value created or preserved. It is defined as Executives Above Target Pay / Executives Above Target Performance; the result is stated in ratio (x:1 or x). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Values outside the intended band, on either side, warrant investigation.

## 3. Context

Pay-for-performance alignment measures the linkage between executive compensation and company results - typically through the compensation actually realized versus target, incentive-plan metric construction, and pay-versus-performance disclosure (now mandated in the US with specific metrics like compensation-actually-paid calculations). The metric is the governance answer to the classic principal-agent problem: executives with weak performance linkage extract value without delivering it. Measurement is genuinely hard: realized-pay ratios conflate market movements with performance (equity awards rise with the whole market), metric selection can be gamed (chosen measures that always pay out), and peer-group benchmarking ratchets pay upward regardless of performance. Read with incentive-metric outcome distributions over multiple years (do metrics actually vary meaningfully?), CEO-pay-ratio trends, and clawback-policy invocation history as revealed seriousness.

## Formula

```
Executives Above Target Pay / Executives Above Target Performance
```
