## 2. Definition

A Learning & Development indicator, Time to Competency (TTC-L) quantifies capability maturity or development. The formula is Days from Role Entry to Competent Performance - reported in days. It is applied across virtually every sector. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

Time to competency measures how long new hires take to reach defined productivity benchmarks, and it is the metric that links onboarding design, knowledge management, and manager coaching quality into one outcome. It is expensive when long: extended ramp time in high-salary roles can cost six figures per hire in unproductive payroll, which is why sales organizations especially invest in structured enablement. The measurement trap is benchmark definition - if competency is self-assessed or manager-guessed, the number drifts; role-specific output measures (first deal closed, tickets resolved without escalation) are the honest version. Read with 90-day and 180-day productivity percentile tracking, early attrition (quits before competency represent total loss), and cohort variation to identify manager-quality differences.
