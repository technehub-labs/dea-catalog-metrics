## 2. Definition

Board Skills Coverage (BSC) captures capability maturity or development for organizations tracking corporate governance. It is defined as Required Competencies Present on Board / Required Competencies; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

Board skills coverage measures the composition of the board against a defined skills matrix - financial expertise, industry knowledge, technology, risk management, international experience - and it is the competence-matching metric that governance codes increasingly push through comply-or-explain disclosure. The metric exists because independence alone is insufficient: an independent board lacking financial or industry expertise cannot challenge management effectively, no matter how conflict-free its members are. The skills-matrix construction carries the gaming risk - a matrix built to match current directors rather than the company's strategic needs makes coverage look complete while the board misses what the future requires (AI governance, cyber risk, climate transition exposure are contemporary examples). Read with skills-matrix refresh cadence against strategy changes, director-recruitment sourcing (skills gaps revealed through search criteria), and board-evaluation findings which should surface where skills are thin.

## Formula

```
Required Competencies Present on Board / Required Competencies
```
