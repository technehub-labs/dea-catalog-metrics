## 2. Definition

Labor Cost Percentage (Restaurants) (LCPR) captures how efficiently resources are converted into outputs for organizations tracking restaurants. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Labor Cost / Food and Beverage Revenue; the result is stated in percent (%). It should be driven down, since lower readings signal healthier performance. In Restaurants, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Labor cost percentage measures labor spend against revenue, and it is one of the two pillars (with food cost) of restaurant profitability - operating typically in the 25-35 percent range, it is the cost line with the most management latitude and the most risk when squeezed. The gaming risk is direct: cutting labor below the service-quality threshold shows up in customer experience scores with a lag, then traffic, making labor cost percentage look good while the business degrades - the classic false economy. Scheduling-technology optimization (matching labor deployment to forecast demand curves) is the legitimate lever, worth multiple points of margin. Read with sales per labor hour, service-speed metrics and guest satisfaction correlation, turnover rate (labor cost includes hiring and training), and minimum-wage exposure by market.

## Formula

```
Labor Cost / Food and Beverage Revenue
```
