## 2. Definition

Within Arts & Culture, Audience Development Rate (ADR-A) is used to gauge growth over time. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Its calculation is First-Time Attendees / Total Attendance, measured in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. It originates from and is most used in Arts & culture practice.

## 3. Context

Audience development rate measures growth in new and returning audiences - particularly first-time attenders and under-engaged demographics - and it is the future-audience metric of arts sustainability: the sector's audience skews old in many markets, and development programs (youth tickets, community partnerships, relaxed performances) exist to broaden the base. The measurement distinguishes new-audience acquisition from existing-audience retention: growth that comes from the same core attending more is deepening, not developing. Demographic-segment tracking is the honest version - development among young, local, and diverse audiences specifically. Read with first-timer conversion to second attendance (the make-or-break retention step), audience demographic trend versus catchment, and development-program cost per new attender.

## Formula

```
ADR-A = First-Time Attendees / Total Attendance
```
