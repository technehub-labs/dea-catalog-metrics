## 2. Definition

Within Sustainability & ESG, Biodiversity Impact Index (BII) is used to gauge environmental and social sustainability performance. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. The formula is Measured Land or Habitat Impact per Operations - reported in index (0-100 or normalized). It should be driven down, since lower readings signal healthier performance. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

A composite quantification of the organization's effect on ecosystems and species - the newest member of the E-value metric family, inheriting the carbon world's measurement ambition without its standardization: no single protocol dominates, and frameworks from the TNFD disclosure recommendations to footprint-based approaches compete for adoption. Its strategic significance is early-mover risk and opportunity: biodiversity loss became a policy target with the global framework's 30-by-30 commitments, and sectors sitting on land-intensive footprints - agriculture, mining, infrastructure - face a disclosure and permitting future that will require some version of this number. The honest use is trend and location: biodiversity is locality-specific in a way carbon is not (a hectare of disturbed habitat is not fungible with another), so a single index should always be read with a map, and materiality analysis should identify the few sites where the impact is concentrated.
