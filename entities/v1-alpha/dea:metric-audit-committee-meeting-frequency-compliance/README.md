## 2. Definition

Within Corporate Governance, Audit Committee Meeting Frequency Compliance (ACMF) is used to gauge conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. It is defined as Audit Committee Meetings Held / Required Meetings; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

Audit committee meeting frequency compliance measures adherence to the required or self-declared number of committee meetings, and it is the audit-quality infrastructure metric - the SEC and listing rules require specific committee responsibilities (hiring and overseeing the auditor, overseeing internal controls), and a committee that meets rarely cannot exercise oversight over a full financial-reporting cycle. The strategic insight is that the audit committee is the single control point between management (who prepare financials) and the auditor (who audit them), so committee capacity - frequency, financial-literacy depth of members, and willingness to challenge - determines the control's real strength. Frequency compliance is a floor metric: meeting the count tells you nothing about the quality of the oversight inside the meetings. Read with financial-expert designation coverage (a mandated committee composition element), private-session frequency with auditors (the structure that enables auditor candor), and auditor-tenure and auditor-change decisions.

## Formula

```
ACMF = Audit Committee Meetings Held / Required Meetings
```
