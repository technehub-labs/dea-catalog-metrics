## 2. Definition

Ancillary Revenue per Passenger (ARPP) measures the economic value created or preserved in the Travel & Tourism domain. It is defined as Ancillary Revenue / Passengers; the result is stated in currency per unit. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. In Travel & tourism, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Ancillary revenue per passenger measures non-ticket income - baggage fees, seat selection, onboard sales, loyalty monetization - divided by passenger count, and it is the airline industry's margin revolution in a single number: carriers like the leading US ultra-low-cost models generate a large share of total revenue from ancillaries, fundamentally changing the business model from flying transportation to retail platforms with wings. The strategic insight is unbundling: separating services that were previously bundled lets prices reflect actual usage while lowering the base fare to stimulate traffic. The gaming risk is definitional - what counts as ancillary varies by carrier (loyalty program income? co-branded card royalties?), making comparisons noisy. Read with ancillary share of total revenue by carrier type, fare versus total revenue per passenger, and attach rates by ancillary product line.

## Formula

```
Ancillary Revenue / Passengers
```
