## 2. Definition

Donor Retention Rate (DRR-N) measures the retention of customers, users, or assets over time in the Nonprofit & NGO domain. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Donors Giving Again within Year / Donors at Start; the result is stated in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. In Nonprofit & NGO, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Donor retention rate measures the share of donors giving again after their first gift, and it is the sustainability crisis metric of the sector - first-time donor retention commonly runs under half in industry benchmarking, meaning most acquisition spending leaks immediately, which makes retention the highest-leverage financial improvement available to most nonprofits. The drivers are known and unglamorous: prompt and personal acknowledgment, reporting on what the gift achieved, and asking again at the right time. Retention economics compound through lifetime value differences by cohort. Read with second-gift conversion (the make-or-break transition), retention by first-gift channel, upgrades versus downgrades within retained donors, and revenue retention versus donor-count retention since big donors moving matter more than counts.

## Formula

```
Donors Giving Again within Year / Donors at Start
```
