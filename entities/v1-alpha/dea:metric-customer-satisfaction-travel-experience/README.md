## 2. Definition

Customer Satisfaction (Travel Experience) (CST) measures the engagement or satisfaction of the people involved in the Travel & Tourism domain. The formula is Satisfied Traveler Survey Responses / Responses - reported in percent (%). It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. It is a standard measure in the Travel & tourism sector.

## 3. Context

Customer satisfaction in travel measures the end-to-end experience - booking through arrival - and it carries distinctive weight in an industry where a single operational failure (a missed connection, lost bag, delayed flight) destroys an otherwise flawless experience. Travel satisfaction is peak-end driven: the final stage (baggage claim, arrival experience) and the worst moment disproportionately determine scores, so improvement investments should target failure-point elimination before polish. NPS-style scoring has dominated travel loyalty economics, and the metric connects directly to repeat booking, ancillary spend, and loyalty program engagement. Read with satisfaction by journey stage, complaint root-cause distribution, on-time performance correlation, and post-recovery satisfaction - service recovery done well can actually outperform no-failure baselines.

Satisfied Traveler Survey Responses / Responses
