## 2. Definition

Within Human Resources, Labor Cost Ratio (LCR) is used to gauge how efficiently resources are converted into outputs. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Total Labor Cost / Revenue; the result is stated in percent (%). The objective is neither maximization nor minimization but staying within an agreed operating band. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

Total labor cost - wages, benefits, and employer taxes - as a share of revenue, the structural weight of people in the business model. Service businesses routinely run above 50 percent; capital-intensive ones below 10, and the level is destiny: a business whose labor ratio is structurally high must compete on labor productivity and pricing, while one that is low competes on capital efficiency. The strategic use is trajectory against growth: labor cost growing faster than revenue is the leading indicator of margin erosion that income statements confirm only quarters later. It also prices flexibility: the ratio reveals how exposed the cost base is to wage inflation, which is why unions negotiate over it and why automation business cases quote it as the baseline.

## Formula

```
Total Labor Cost / Revenue
```
