## 2. Definition

Cost to Raise a Dollar (CTR) measures how efficiently resources are converted into outputs in the Nonprofit & NGO domain. Its calculation is Fundraising Cost / Funds Raised, measured in ratio (x:1 or x). It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Lower values are better; each reduction is an improvement. It originates from and is most used in Nonprofit & NGO practice.

## 3. Context

Cost to raise a dollar measures fundraising expense divided by funds raised, and it is the inverse-efficiency figure that donors understand instantly - a dollar spent to raise a dollar is break-even theater. Its measurement honesty problem is allocation: joint campaigns, events with dual program-fundraising purpose, and in-kind fundraising all require allocation judgment, so cross-organization comparison needs methodology alignment. The threshold mythology deserves skepticism: rigid benchmarks (never spend more than 25 cents per dollar) starve growth investment, because acquiring new donors costs more than keeping old ones. Read with the acquisition-versus-renewal split (acquisition legitimately costs more), multi-year donor lifetime value rather than single-year ratios, and event-based fundraising cost intensity which carries its own economics.

## Formula

```
Fundraising Cost / Funds Raised
```
