## 2. Definition

A Corporate Governance indicator, Board Meeting Attendance Rate (BMAR) quantifies conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. It is defined as Director Attendances / Seats on Meetings Held; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

Board meeting attendance measures director presence at scheduled meetings, and it is the most basic governance-hygiene metric - directors who miss meetings cannot fulfill fiduciary duties, and chronic absence signals disengagement that undermines the board's monitoring function entirely. The metric has real teeth beyond optics: proxy advisors and institutional investors treat sustained absence as a red flag, some jurisdictions and listing standards now tie board service minimums to attendance, and sustained under-attendance has appeared in activist campaigns. The honest measurement is per-director and meeting-difficulty adjusted: attendance by video counts as attendance in most modern frameworks, and emergency meetings create different constraints than scheduled ones. Read with preparation quality metrics (attendance is necessary but far from sufficient), committee participation depth, and individual director engagement indicators.

## Formula

```
Director Attendances / Seats on Meetings Held
```
