## 2. Definition

A Sustainability & ESG indicator, Renewable Energy Share (RES) quantifies environmental and social sustainability performance. It is defined as Renewable Energy / Total Energy Consumed; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

The proportion of energy consumed that comes from renewable sources - the metric through which corporate climate commitments became arithmetic, and the one that grew from a niche disclosure into a procurement-driven market force: power purchase agreements signed to move this percentage are among the largest corporate drivers of new renewable generation capacity. The strategic honesty question is instrument quality: physically-sited renewable supply, contract-backed purchases, and unbundled certificates all move the headline share with very different real-world effect, and the market-based Scope 2 accounting that matches this share inherits exactly that ambiguity. Read alongside additionality claims: a share built on certificates from projects that would exist anyway measures accounting, not impact, which is why leading frameworks increasingly distinguish between the two in scoring.

## Formula

```
Renewable Energy / Total Energy Consumed
```
