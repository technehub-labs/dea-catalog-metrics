## 2. Definition

Within Sustainability & ESG, Greenhouse Gas Scope 1 Emissions (GHG1) is used to gauge environmental and social sustainability performance. The formula is Direct Emissions from Owned or Controlled Sources - reported in tonnes CO2 equivalent (tCO2e). It is applied across virtually every sector. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

Direct greenhouse gas emissions from sources the organization owns or controls - fuel burned in its boilers, furnaces, and vehicles - the first and least ambiguous tier of the carbon-accounting framework that the GHG Protocol standardized. Scope 1 is the metric most directly under operational control: it moves only when fuel choice, process design, or fleet electrification changes, which makes it the natural object of reduction targets. Strategically it anchors disclosure regimes worldwide, from emissions trading systems (where allowances are denominated in these tonnes) to the CSRD-era reporting that converts carbon from a reputational narrative into an audited balance-sheet-adjacent number. Measurement is comparatively clean - fuel volumes times published emission factors - with the honest caveats around leaks of fugitive gases (methane in oil and gas, refrigerants everywhere), which are the Scope 1 lines most often revised.
