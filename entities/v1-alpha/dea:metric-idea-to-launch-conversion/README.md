## 2. Definition

Idea-to-Launch Conversion (I2L) captures how effectively a process or entity achieves its intended outcome for organizations tracking r&d and innovation. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. The formula is Ideas Reaching Launch / Ideas Submitted - reported in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

The share of submitted ideas that survive to launched products - the funnel-efficiency metric of innovation management, and the one that exposes the structural tension in every idea pipeline: funnels must be brutal to be affordable, but brutal funnels that kill too early discard the weak-signal ideas that were the best ones all along. Its strategic significance is the balance it measures: conversion near zero means the front end collects noise and the process is a suggestion box; conversion near one means the gate discipline is theater and everything ships. Read the funnel stage-by-stage: losses concentrated at ideation-to-concept indicate weak intake curation, at concept-to-development indicate business-case stringency, and at development-to-launch indicate execution and scaling problems - three different organizational failures wearing the same headline rate. The metric only improves with feedback loops, since submitters stop submitting when they cannot see what happened to their ideas.

## Formula

```
Ideas Reaching Launch / Ideas Submitted
```
