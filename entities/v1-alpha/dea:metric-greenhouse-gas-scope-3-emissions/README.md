## 2. Definition

Greenhouse Gas Scope 3 Emissions (GHG3) addresses environmental and social sustainability performance and is a fixture of sustainability & esg reporting. Its calculation is All Other Indirect Value-Chain Emissions, measured in tonnes CO2 equivalent (tCO2e). It is applied across virtually every sector. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

All other indirect emissions across the value chain - purchased goods and services, business travel, transportation, product use, waste, and investments - the tier that typically dwarfs Scopes 1 and 2 combined, often by an order of magnitude. Its strategic significance follows from that dominance: for most products the overwhelming majority of lifecycle carbon sits in either supply (upstream) or product use (downstream), so organizations serious about net-zero commitments cannot act without measurement here, and they cannot measure it precisely - Scope 3 is built from spend data, industry averages, and supplier disclosures, which makes it estimation stacked on estimation. Read the category split before any trend: category 1 (purchased goods) and category 11 (use of sold products) usually carry nearly everything, and targets aimed at the minor categories are theater.
