## 2. Definition

Within Project Management, On-Time Project Delivery (OTPD) is used to gauge conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. It is defined as Projects Delivered On Schedule / Projects Completed; the result is stated in percent (%). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The share of projects delivered by their committed date - the punctuality metric of the portfolio, and the one stakeholders feel most directly because commitments were made with their names attached. Its strategic content is commitment reliability: an organization delivering 60 percent on time is not slow, it is unpredictable, and unpredictability is costlier than slowness because downstream plans built on promised dates fail with it. The research consensus from estimating theory is that misses are mostly committed at approval, not caused during execution - optimistic durations agreed to win approval become schedule facts no amount of execution excellence recovers. Read with the scope-change log: on-time delivery with heavy scope sacrifice is a different signal than on-time with scope intact, and the metric alone does not say which.

## Formula

```
Projects Delivered On Schedule / Projects Completed
```
