## 2. Definition

Within R&D and Innovation, R&D Cost Recovery (RDCC) is used to gauge the economic value created or preserved. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Revenue from New Products / Cumulative R&D Investment; the result is stated in ratio (x:1 or x). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

The share of research and development expenditure recovered through the revenue it generates - the direct-return metric that answers the bluntest question about R&D: did the money come back. Its strategic significance is portfolio accountability: development programs and governments alike increasingly demand recovery evidence for innovation spending, and organizations that cannot demonstrate recovery discover their R&D treated as cost rather than investment. The measurement challenge is attribution - revenue from products born of specific projects arrives years later and mixes with brand and channel effects, so credible recovery analysis uses product-portfolio cohorting rather than project-by-project tracing. Read with the time window: recovery measured over too short a horizon systematically understates, since commercialization lag is years, and the honest metric quotes recovery per cohort at a fixed maturity.

## Formula

```
Revenue from New Products / Cumulative R&D Investment
```
