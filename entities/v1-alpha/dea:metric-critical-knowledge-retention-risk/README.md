## 2. Definition

Within Knowledge Management, Critical Knowledge Retention Risk (CKRR) is used to gauge exposure to adverse outcomes. It is defined as Critical Knowledge Held by Departing or Single Holders / Critical Knowledge Areas; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Reduction is the goal, and sustained decreases indicate successful intervention.

## 3. Context

Measures exposure to loss of knowledge held by few people - expert concentration, undocumented processes, single points of human failure. As workforces turn over faster, this becomes a resilience metric, not a training metric. Quantify by criticality-weighted concentrations (how many people can do this, how vital is it), and keep the inventory current or it degrades into a stale taxonomy. Read with key-person dependency ratio and time-to-competency for replacements.

## Formula

```
Critical Knowledge Held by Departing or Single Holders / Critical Knowledge Areas
```
