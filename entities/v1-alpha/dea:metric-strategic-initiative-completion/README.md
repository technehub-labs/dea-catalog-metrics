## 2. Definition

Strategic Initiative Completion (SIC) measures conformance to standards, plans, or obligations in the Strategy & Portfolio domain. It is defined as Initiatives Completed / Initiatives Planned; the result is stated in percent (%). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The share of strategic initiatives completed on their committed timeline - the execution view of strategy delivery, distinguished from goal attainment by measuring the workstreams rather than their outcomes. Its strategic content is the translation test: strategy documents that spawn initiatives which then stall at 60 percent completion indicate the strategy was never resourced, or the operating model absorbs change poorly, or the initiatives were ill-conceived - and the metric cannot distinguish these, only flag that the gap exists. The honest measurement separates active from silently deprioritized initiatives, since portfolios that quietly stop talking about stalled work will show healthy completion among survivors. Read with capacity utilization of the change organization: chronic overload produces the pattern of everything 80 percent complete that this metric surfaces.

## Formula

```
Initiatives Completed / Initiatives Planned
```
