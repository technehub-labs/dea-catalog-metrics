## 2. Definition

Research Headcount Ratio (RHR) captures capability maturity or development for organizations tracking r&d and innovation. Its calculation is R&D Personnel / Total Personnel, measured in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Values outside the intended band, on either side, warrant investigation.

## 3. Context

The share of the workforce employed in research and development - the human-capital companion to R&D intensity, and in talent-driven sectors the more binding constraint: budget can be raised in a quarter, while a researcher pipeline takes years to build, which is why this ratio moves glacially even when R&D budgets swing. Its strategic significance is capability signaling: the ratio tracks whether the organization is structurally a research organization or a company that funds research, and the trend reveals whether stated innovation ambition is being staffed or merely announced. Read with the composition: research staff embedded in business units versus central laboratories behave differently (proximity to market versus depth), and the ratio of PhD-qualified staff within the research population is the quality dimension the headcount count cannot see.

## Formula

```
R&D Personnel / Total Personnel
```
