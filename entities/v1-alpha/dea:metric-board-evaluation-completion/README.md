## 2. Definition

Board Evaluation Completion (BEC) measures conformance to standards, plans, or obligations in the Corporate Governance domain. It is computed as Board Evaluations Completed on Schedule / Boards and Committees Requiring Evaluation, expressed in percent (%). It is a headline indicator: movement in it prompts leadership questions about strategy and resourcing. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

Board evaluation completion measures whether the board conducts and completes its periodic evaluation process - now mandated or strongly encouraged by governance codes in most jurisdictions - and it is the self-improvement-infrastructure metric of governance. The disclosure regimes increasingly require evaluation (UK code requires annual evaluation, externally facilitated at least every three years), making completion a compliance floor. The honest read is depth-sensitive: evaluations range from questionnaire-theater to genuine director-candor interviews with external facilitators, and the completion metric cannot distinguish them - which is precisely why codes now push external facilitation as a quality marker. The action test is the real measure: evaluations identify issues (skill gaps, dynamics problems, underperforming directors) that should produce observable change. Read with evaluation-action follow-through (issue identification versus change), director-refresh turnover correlation, and external-facilitation cadence.

## Formula

```
Board Evaluations Completed on Schedule / Boards and Committees Requiring Evaluation
```
