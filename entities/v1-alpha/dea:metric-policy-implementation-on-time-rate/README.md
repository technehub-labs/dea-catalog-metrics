## 2. Definition

Policy Implementation On-Time Rate (PIT) measures conformance to standards, plans, or obligations in the Public Sector & Government domain. Its calculation is Policy Milestones Met on Schedule / Milestones Planned, measured in percent (%). It originates from and is most used in Public sector practice. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

The share of policy initiatives implemented by their committed date - the delivery-discipline metric of government, whose strategic significance is the credibility chain between legislation and citizen experience: policy announced and not implemented is a promise with no service attached, and the gap between adoption and implementation is where policy intentions quietly die. The research tradition on implementation failure (the classic political-science finding) is that the gap is structural - implementation requires capacity, coordination, and incentives that the announcement itself never provides - and this metric is the accountability that forces the machinery to acknowledge it. The honest read separates the genuinely-delayed from the silently-abandoned: initiatives that miss every milestone without ever being formally stopped are the pattern that makes the on-time rate more honest than a status report, since a date is a fact and an in-progress status is a feeling.

## Formula

```
Policy Milestones Met on Schedule / Milestones Planned
```
