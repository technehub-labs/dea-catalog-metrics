## 2. Definition

Care Recipient Satisfaction (CRS) measures the engagement or satisfaction of the people involved in the Elder & Social Care domain. It is a headline indicator: movement in it prompts leadership questions about strategy and resourcing. It is computed as Satisfied Recipient or Family Survey Responses / Responses, expressed in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Its use is concentrated in Social care.

## 3. Context

Care recipient satisfaction measures the experienced quality of care from the resident or family perspective, and in elder care it carries the distinctive challenge that many care recipients have cognitive impairment, requiring adapted measurement (proxy surveys, observation-based tools) with their own validity debates. The metric matters commercially and regulatorily: consumer-review platforms and star ratings incorporate it, and family choice drives census. Satisfaction in care settings correlates with specific drivers more than ambiance: staff responsiveness, dignity and autonomy preservation, food quality, and communication with families, and the pain points cluster around call-light response and staffing visibility. Read with satisfaction by family versus resident (they diverge meaningfully), complaint themes versus satisfaction items (the triangulation), and longitudinal trend at the unit level where management quality shows.

## Formula

```
Satisfied Recipient or Family Survey Responses / Responses
```
