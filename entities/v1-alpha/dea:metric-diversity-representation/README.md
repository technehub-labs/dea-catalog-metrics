## 2. Definition

Within Human Resources, Diversity Representation (DiR) is used to gauge conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. It is defined as Underrepresented Group Headcount / Total Headcount; the result is stated in percent (%). It is applied across virtually every sector. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

The demographic composition of the workforce - conventionally the share of underrepresented groups at each level, tracked against the population or applicant pool. Its strategic significance is the pipeline shape: representation that is healthy at entry and thin at senior levels indicates the problem is promotion and retention, not attraction, and interventions belong at the boundary between levels where the drop-off occurs. Reporting regimes increasingly mandate disclosure of the metric, which has moved it from values statement to compliance item in most large markets. The honest measurement requires both a denominator (internal levels) and a reference (external availability), since a figure that looks low against the population may match the qualified labor pool - and disaggregation matters, since blended diversity percentages can conceal divergent trends between groups.

## Formula

```
Underrepresented Group Headcount / Total Headcount
```
