## 2. Definition

Benefits Realization Ratio (BRR) measures the economic value created or preserved in the Project Management domain. It informs long-horizon decisions and is typically tracked on enterprise dashboards rather than team boards. Its calculation is Realized Benefits / Planned Benefits, measured in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. Practitioners in nearly any industry can adopt it directly.

## 3. Context

The value actually realized from projects against the benefits promised in their business cases - the closing of the loop that separates project management from investment management. Its strategic weight is credibility economics: organizations that never check realization accumulate a portfolio of optimistic cases whose patterns repeat, and the next business case is written against that culture; organizations that audit realization learn which benefit types are predictable and which are fiction, and their next portfolio is better allocated. Measurement requires pinning benefits at approval with owners, baselines, and measurement dates - benefits attached to the project die with the project manager unless transferred to a P&L owner. Ratios below two-thirds are the norm in first-year measurement programs, which is itself the finding that motivates the discipline.

## Formula

```
Realized Benefits / Planned Benefits
```
