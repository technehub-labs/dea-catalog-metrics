## 2. Definition

Placement Retention at 90 Days (PR90) measures the quality of outputs and outcomes in the Staffing & Recruiting domain. It is defined as Placements Retained at 90 Days / Placements Made; the result is stated in percent (%). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. In Staffing & recruiting, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Placement retention at 90 days measures the share of placed candidates still on the job after three months, and it is the quality-versus-speed referee of staffing - placements that fail in the first quarter are usually unpaid (guarantee periods), damage client relationships, and signal screening shortcuts. The 90-day window is chosen deliberately: early-tenure failures are much more likely screening or expectation-mismatch problems than later-tenure failures (which tend to reflect workplace conditions beyond the agency's control). Retention quality is the metric that separates sustainable agencies from churn-driven ones - an agency that maximizes placement speed while accepting poor retention spends more on refunds and re-work than it saves on screening. Read with client repeat-engagement rate (retention quality predicts repeat business), placement speed, and reason-coded exit analysis.

## Formula

```
PR90 = Placements Retained at 90 Days / Placements Made
```
