## 2. Definition

A Airport Operations indicator, Passenger Satisfaction (Airport) (PSA) quantifies the engagement or satisfaction of the people involved. It is defined as Satisfied Passenger Survey Responses / Responses; the result is stated in percent (%). In Airports, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

Passenger satisfaction measures the terminal experience end to end - arrival, check-in, security, wayfinding, amenities, and departure - and it is the commercial-foundation metric for airports whose revenue increasingly comes from non-aeronautical sources (retail, dining, parking) that scale with dwell time and mood. The satisfaction drivers are asymmetric: security wait and cleanliness dominate the downside while amenities drive the upside, so investment sequencing follows the pattern of fixing pain points first. Airport satisfaction is also flow-sensitive: a smooth terminal scores well even with modest amenities, while chaos degrades everything. Read with satisfaction by journey stage, NPS among connecting versus origin passengers (they experience different airports), dwell-time trend (commercial revenue opportunity), and complaint root-cause distribution.
