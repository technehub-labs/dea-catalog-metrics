## 2. Definition

Student Retention Rate (SRR) captures the retention of customers, users, or assets over time for organizations tracking education. It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. The formula is Students Continuing / Students Enrolled at Start - reported in percent (%). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. It is a standard measure in the Education sector.

## 3. Context

The share of students who continue from one period to the next - the institutional metric where education economics live, since in nearly every funding model the student who stays is the revenue, and the student who leaves takes subsidized recruitment and first-year delivery costs with them without paying them back. Its strategic significance is the early-warning structure: attrition concentrates in the first year, and the metrics that predict it - first-term performance, engagement, financial stress - are all observable while there is still time to intervene, which is why retention programs are early-alert systems more than marketing programs. The honest analysis is cohort-based and reason-coded: transfers, stop-outs, and permanent attrition are different events with different remedies, and an institution that cannot split them cannot act on its own number.

## Formula

```
Students Continuing / Students Enrolled at Start
```
