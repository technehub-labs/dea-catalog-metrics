## 2. Definition

Beneficiary Reach (BFR) addresses the utilization or availability of capacity and is a fixture of nonprofit & ngo reporting. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Individuals Served per Period; the result is stated in count (items per period). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. In Nonprofit & NGO, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

Beneficiary reach measures the number of people served by programs, and it is the scale metric of nonprofit delivery - the answer to the mission-scope question that underlies all funder conversations. Its honesty problem is definitional: a person touched once by a workshop and a person in a year-long program both count as one beneficiary, so reach figures without depth or dosage are nearly meaningless, which is why serious organizations report reach alongside service intensity. Reach is also the denominator of cost-per-beneficiary, making it self-interested - inflating reach deflates cost figures. Read with depth-of-service measures (contact hours, program completion), outcome attainment rates among those reached, and cost per beneficiary adjusted for program intensity.
