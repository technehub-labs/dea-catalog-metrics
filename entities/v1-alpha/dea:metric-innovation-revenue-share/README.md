## 2. Definition

Innovation Revenue Share (IRS) measures growth over time in the R&D and Innovation domain. It is defined as Revenue from Products Launched in Last 3 Years / Total Revenue; the result is stated in percent (%). Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The share of revenue from products or services launched within a defined recent window - the innovation-output metric that answers the question R&D intensity cannot: whether the investment became money. Its strategic significance is the portfolio-honesty it forces: the classic finding of innovation research (Cooper's work, among others) is that a small share of development projects produce most new-product profit, and measuring the revenue side exposes the hit concentration that input metrics hide. The definitional choice is the whole game: what counts as new (new-to-world versus new-to-firm versus relaunch) and the window length move the number enormously, so the metric only carries information trended under a fixed definition. Read with margin, since innovation revenue at cannibalized margins is substitution wearing growth's clothes - the failure mode that pure revenue-share metrics reward.

## Formula

```
Revenue from Products Launched in Last 3 Years / Total Revenue
```
