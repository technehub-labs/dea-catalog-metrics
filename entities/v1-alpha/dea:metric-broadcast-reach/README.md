## 2. Definition

Broadcast Reach (BCR) captures the economic value created or preserved for organizations tracking sports & events. It is defined as Average Audience per Broadcast; the result is stated in count (items per period). In Sports & events, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

Broadcast reach measures the audience exposed to a broadcast - unique viewers rather than average audience - and it is the currency metric of media rights deals, where leagues and networks negotiate on the size of the reachable audience. Reach differs fundamentally from average viewership in what it signals: a show can have modest average audience but broad reach (sampled by many, watched through by few), which matters for advertising exposure metrics and for rights-fee justification. The fragmentation problem is the metric's central challenge - streaming, clips, and social distribution split the audience across platforms, so total reach now requires cross-platform measurement rather than broadcast-only panels. Read with average audience versus reach decomposition, demographic reach against advertiser targets, time-spent trends, and cross-platform unduplicated audience measurement.
