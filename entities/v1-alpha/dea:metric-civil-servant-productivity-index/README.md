## 2. Definition

Civil Servant Productivity Index (CSPI) addresses how effectively a process or entity achieves its intended outcome and is a fixture of public sector & government reporting. Its trend is watched by senior leadership as an early signal of structural performance shifts. It is defined as Weighted Index of Throughput, Timeliness, Satisfaction; the result is stated in index (0-100 or normalized). Improvement means raising this figure toward best-in-class levels. In Public sector, it is a recognized element of the standard performance toolkit.

## 3. Context

A composite of output per civil servant across government functions - the productivity metric that public administration tracks carefully because the workforce is its largest controllable cost, and the honest version is a task-basis composite: services processed, cases resolved, and payments administered per full-time-equivalent, since the private-sector revenue-per-employee logic does not transfer to an organization that does not sell. Its strategic significance is workforce-sizing reality: salary bills drive fiscal pressure, and a productivity index is the counter-argument evidence for the debate between workforce-cutting and capacity-investing approaches - each of which fails without it. The measurement honesty requirement is output-quality pairing: productivity that rises while case backlog and error rates rise is throughput wearing productivity's clothes, so the index is always published with quality and timeliness companions. Read by function, since a government-wide index is an average over activities too different to average.

## Formula

```
Weighted Index of Throughput, Timeliness, Satisfaction
```
