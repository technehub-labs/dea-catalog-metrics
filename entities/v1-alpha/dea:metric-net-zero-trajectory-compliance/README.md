## 2. Definition

Within Sustainability & ESG, Net Zero Trajectory Compliance (NZTC) is used to gauge conformance to standards, plans, or obligations. The formula is Actual Emissions / Science-Based Target Pathway - reported in ratio (x:1 or x). It carries weight in boardroom and executive reviews, where it anchors cross-organization trade-offs. Higher values are better; gains are read as improvement and losses as deterioration. Its use is not sector-specific; it appears in cross-industry performance frameworks.

## 3. Context

The degree to which the organization's emissions trajectory and planned measures align with its net-zero commitment - typically assessed against interim milestones, carbon-budget arithmetic, and the credibility of the reliance on removals and offsets. Its strategic weight is the gap it is designed to expose: the research tradition tracking pledges finds most sectors off-trajectory, and compliance assessment converts a distant promise (net zero by 2050) into near-term accountability (what must be true by 2030). The methodological battleground is the residual: what share of remaining emissions will be abated versus neutralized by removals, since net-zero frameworks constrain removals to a small balancing share, and a plan that leans on offsets is not compliant however the word is used. Read with capital-allocation evidence: a trajectory is credible when the capex behind it appears in approved plans, not in ambition statements.

## Formula

```
Actual Emissions / Science-Based Target Pathway
```
