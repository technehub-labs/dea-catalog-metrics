## 2. Definition

Earnings Guidance Hit Rate (EGHR) addresses the quality of outputs and outcomes and is a fixture of investor relations & capital markets reporting. It is computed as Periods within Guidance Range / Periods Guided, expressed in percent (%). Its use is concentrated in Listed companies. It is a headline indicator: movement in it prompts leadership questions about strategy and resourcing. It should be maximized within the limits of cost and quality trade-offs.

## 3. Context

The share of guidance ranges met - the credibility metric of the investor-relations function, and one of the few IR numbers with a measurable market consequence: the research tradition on guidance shows that missing your own stated range is punished beyond the miss's economic content, because the market prices the reliability of the signal, not just the signal. Its strategic significance is the cumulative-trust economics: a company that guides and hits builds a discount rate on its announcements, one that misses raises its cost of being believed, and the hit rate compounds in either direction. The honest read separates guidance philosophy from execution: ranges deliberately set sandbagged are hit cheaply, and the market learns to gross them up - so the metric is most meaningful read alongside the surprise distribution, where a suspiciously high hit rate with uniformly modest beats is a company managing expectations rather than managing the business.

## Formula

```
Periods within Guidance Range / Periods Guided
```
