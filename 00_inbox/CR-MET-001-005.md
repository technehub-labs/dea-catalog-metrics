OpenDEAM Metric Capability — CR-MET-001 to CR-MET-005

These five CRs will establish the foundational Metric and Measurement architecture within OpenDEAM.

I am treating them as implementation-grade Change Requests, not conceptual proposals. Each will have:

* A defined architectural objective and scope.
* Explicit metamodel changes.
* Entity definitions and semantic boundaries.
* Attributes, relationships and cardinality requirements.
* Repository and artifact changes.
* CI/CD, validation and conformance requirements.
* Migration and backward-compatibility considerations.
* Acceptance criteria and Definition of Done.
* Dependencies and downstream implications.

Programme conventions

Before the individual CRs, three existing architectural decisions must remain binding.

1. Authority boundaries

The earlier CR-10J work established Metric with definition, unit, calculation, source, baseline and target. The CR-10 Outcome model also references metric, baseline, expected outcome, target, unit, confidence, timeframe and evidence.

These concepts must be reconciled rather than recreated.

In particular, baseline and target should not automatically become intrinsic properties of Metric. They may vary by organizational subject, reporting period, strategic objective or measurement context.

3. Canonical specification takes precedence

The normative metamodel source is:

The generated registries include:

* metamodel/registry/entities.yaml
* metamodel/registry/relationships.yaml

The CRs must follow the existing repository’s authoritative specification, schema, registry and CI/CD conventions. They must not introduce competing definitions or manually maintained generated registry entries.

CR-MET-001 — Establish the Canonical Metric Entity

CR-MET-001

Foundation

Title: Establish Metric as a First-Class OpenDEAM Metamodel Entity

Target repository: technehub-labs/dea-metamodel

Priority: Critical

Change type: Metamodel extension / semantic foundation

Depends on: CR-MET-000 reconciliation; existing identity and governance contracts.

1. Background and problem statement

OpenDEAM requires a canonical, reusable representation of a Metric.

Although the earlier CR-10J work introduced Metric into the conceptual architecture, the metamodel must establish its complete semantic identity, formal attributes, relationships, constraints and lifecycle expectations.

Without this, different catalogs and architectural domains could independently define metrics with inconsistent meanings.

For example:

* One catalog may define Customer Retention Rate as a percentage.
* Another may define it as the number of retained customers.
* A third may define it as the percentage of customers retained over a rolling twelve-month period.

Although they share a name, these may represent materially different metrics.

OpenDEAM must distinguish semantic identity from display names and establish the information necessary to determine whether two metric definitions are equivalent.

2. Objective

Establish Metric as a first-class, independently identifiable, reusable semantic entity in the OpenDEAM metamodel.

The entity must:

* Describe what is intended to be measured.
* Define the measurement’s semantic meaning.
* Establish how the metric is determined.
* Identify its measurement unit or measurement scale.
* Identify its measurement source or method.
* Support association with enterprise entities.
* Support reusable definitions across multiple contexts.
* Support versioning and governance.
* Remain independent of any specific performance-management framework.

3. Canonical semantic definition

Metric

A Metric is a formally defined, reusable semantic specification that describes a measurable property, characteristic, condition, quantity, rate, ratio, index or other observable aspect of a subject, including the semantic rules necessary to determine and interpret its value.

A Metric defines what is to be measured and the rules governing its determination. It does not, by itself, represent a measurement execution or an observed result.

This distinction is normative.

4. Proposed entity specification

Entity: Metric

Attribute	Requirement	Description
id	Required	Canonical entity identifier
name	Required	Human-readable metric name
definition	Required	Formal semantic definition
description	Recommended	Extended explanatory description
purpose	Recommended	Intended measurement purpose
subject_concept	Required	Type of phenomenon or property measured
measurement_unit	Conditional	Unit or scale applicable to the metric
measurement_method	Recommended	Method by which the metric is determined
calculation_definition	Conditional	Formula or computational definition
measurement_source	Recommended	Expected source or source category
classification	Required	Metric taxonomy classification
measurement_type	Required	Numeric, ordinal, nominal, Boolean or other supported type
directionality	Recommended	Desired interpretation of increasing/decreasing values
applicability	Recommended	Applicable subjects and contexts
owner	Governance-controlled	Accountable owner
steward	Governance-controlled	Semantic steward
lifecycle_status	Governance-controlled	Lifecycle state
version	Governance-controlled	Definition version

The field names are proposed logical attributes; the implementation must map them to existing canonical naming and identity conventions.

Important semantic constraints

* A Metric must have a stable identity independent of its name.
* A Metric must have an unambiguous definition.
* A Metric must identify the subject concept it measures.
* A Metric must declare an appropriate measurement type.
* Numeric metrics must define or reference a compatible unit or scale.
* Formula-based metrics must have an explicit calculation definition.
* Qualitative metrics must not be forced into numeric measurement semantics.
* A Metric must not contain individual observation values.
* A Metric must not contain operational measurement history.

5. Existing Metric reconciliation

The CR must explicitly reconcile CR-10J.

The migration must not silently remove existing Outcome semantics.

6. Relationships

The Metric entity must support the following semantic relationships.

Related entity	Relationship meaning
Strategic Objective	Metric measures objective-related performance

These relationships must use the existing OpenDEAM relationship architecture, not ad hoc references.

Cardinality must allow one Metric to be reused across multiple subjects and multiple metrics to describe one subject.

CR-MET-002 — Establish Metric Classification and Taxonomy

CR-MET-002

Taxonomy

Title: Establish the Canonical Metric Classification Framework

Target repository: dea-metamodel

Priority: High

Depends on: CR-MET-001 and relevant classification architecture.

1. Background and problem statement

Metric definitions without a consistent classification framework become difficult to discover, compare, govern and reuse.

Enterprise metrics are used for different purposes and describe different subjects.

For example:

* Revenue growth is a financial performance metric.
* Customer churn is a customer and retention metric.
* Mean time to recovery is an operational and technical reliability metric.
* Employee engagement is an organizational metric.
* Data completeness is a data quality metric.

These classifications are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

OpenDEAM therefore requires a multidimensional taxonomy rather than a rigid hierarchy that forces every Metric into one category.

2. Objective

Establish a canonical, extensible Metric classification framework supporting discovery, semantic grouping, governance, analytics and interoperability.

3. Proposed classification dimensions

A. Measurement subject

Identifies what the Metric measures.

Classification	Examples
Financial	Revenue, margin, cost
Customer	Satisfaction, retention, acquisition
Organizational	Engagement, workforce stability
Operational	Throughput, efficiency, cycle time
Technology	Availability, latency, reliability
Data	Accuracy, completeness, consistency
Risk	Exposure, loss frequency
Capability	Capability performance and maturity
Strategic	Strategic objective attainment
Sustainability	Environmental and social performance

This classification must be extensible. It should not imply that all Metric instances belong to exactly one subject category.

B. Measurement purpose

Identifies why the Metric is used.

* Performance measurement.
* Risk monitoring.
* Compliance monitoring.
* Quality measurement.
* Efficiency measurement.
* Effectiveness measurement.
* Maturity measurement.
* Outcome measurement.
* Diagnostic measurement.
* Predictive measurement.

C. Measurement orientation

Type	Meaning
Leading	Provides an indication of potential future conditions
Lagging	Records an outcome that has already occurred
Diagnostic	Helps explain a condition or performance result
Concurrent	Describes a condition occurring during the measurement period

D. Measurement nature

* Quantitative.
* Qualitative.
* Categorical.
* Ordinal.
* Binary.
* Composite.

E. Measurement scope

* Enterprise.
* Business domain.
* Business unit.
* Organizational unit.
* Value stream.
* Capability.
* Process.
* Product or service.
* System or component.
* Individual subject, where authorized and appropriate.

F. Management significance

* General Metric.
* Key Performance Indicator (KPI).
* Key Risk Indicator (KRI).
* Key Control Indicator (KCI).
* Service-Level Indicator (SLI).
* Other governed indicator designations.

These should be designations or governed classifications, not automatic entity specializations.

4. Taxonomy architecture

The implementation must distinguish:

Classification scheme: Defines a taxonomy or controlled vocabulary.

Classification concept: Represents an individual classification value.

Classification assignment: Associates a Metric with one or more classification concepts.

The preferred implementation should reuse the existing OpenDEAM classification metamodel, rather than create a Metric-specific classification mechanism.

Where multiple classification schemes apply, their assignments must remain independently identifiable.

5. Classification constraints

* A Metric may have multiple subject classifications.
* A Metric may have multiple purpose classifications.
* A Metric may have multiple orientation classifications only where the definitions genuinely support them.
* A Metric must have a measurement-nature classification.
* A Metric may carry multiple management designations.
* Classification concepts must be governed and versioned.
* Deprecated classifications must remain historically traceable.
* Classification must not alter the fundamental identity of a Metric.

6. Implementation tasks

* Review existing OpenDEAM classification mechanisms.
* Identify reusable taxonomy structures.
* Define canonical Metric classification schemes.
* Define classification concepts and identifiers.
* Establish classification assignment relationships.
* Define controlled vocabulary governance.
* Establish extension and specialization rules.
* Add representative classified Metric definitions.
* Implement validation for mandatory and optional classifications.
* Generate classification documentation.
* Add cross-domain taxonomy examples.

The CR must include at least these representative Metric definitions:

Employee Engagement Score	Organizational	Diagnostic	Concurrent	Ordinal or composite, depending on methodology
Mean Time to Recovery	Technology / Operations	Reliability	Lagging	Quantitative

Strategic Objective Attainment	Strategic	Outcome	Lagging	Ordinal or quantitative, depending on definition
Operational Capacity Utilization	Operations	Efficiency	Concurrent	Quantitative

8. Definition of Done

* Existing classification architecture reviewed.
* Canonical Metric taxonomy established.
* Classification schemes and concepts documented.
* Multiple classification assignments supported.
* Management designations defined.
* Extension and deprecation rules established.
* Classification validation implemented.
* Representative examples validated.
* No competing Metric-specific classification architecture introduced.

CR-MET-003 — Establish Measurement and Observation Entities

CR-MET-003

Core measurement model

Title: Establish Measurement and Observation as Distinct OpenDEAM Entities

Target repository: dea-metamodel

Priority: Critical

Depends on: CR-MET-001, CR-MET-002 and identity architecture.

1. Background and problem statement

A Metric definition alone cannot represent what actually happened when an enterprise measures something.

OpenDEAM must distinguish:

* The definition of what should be measured.
* The application of that definition to a subject.
* The result obtained.
* The evidence supporting that result.

Without these distinctions, the metamodel cannot reliably support historical measurement, traceability, comparisons, trend analysis or measurement provenance.

2. Objective

Introduce two complementary entities:

Measurement: Represents a defined instance of applying a Metric to a subject within a specified context, period and measurement configuration.

Observation: Represents a recorded value or result produced or captured through a Measurement.

These entities must support multiple measurements and observations against one reusable Metric definition.

3. Canonical semantic definitions

Measurement

A Measurement is an identifiable occurrence or record of applying a Metric definition to a particular subject, within a defined context, using an identified measurement method and applicable measurement configuration.

Observation

An Observation is an identifiable record of an observed or determined value associated with a Metric and its measurement context, including the temporal, provenance and quality information necessary to interpret that value.

The distinction is normative.

4. Proposed entity: Measurement

Attribute	Requirement	Description
id	Required	Measurement identifier
metric	Required	Reference to canonical Metric
subject	Required	Entity being measured
measurement_context	Recommended	Context of measurement
measurement_method	Required	Method applied
measurement_time	Required	Time of measurement execution or capture
reporting_period	Conditional	Period represented by the measurement
measurement_agent	Recommended	Human, system or process responsible
measurement_source	Recommended	Data source or originating system
measurement_status	Required	Execution or record status
configuration_reference	Recommended	Versioned measurement configuration
evidence_reference	Recommended	Supporting evidence

A Measurement must reference a specific Metric definition version or otherwise preserve the exact semantic version used.

5. Proposed entity: Observation

Attribute	Requirement	Description
id	Required	Observation identifier
metric	Required	Metric reference
measurement	Required	Parent Measurement reference
observed_value	Required	Actual value or categorical result
observed_unit	Conditional	Unit of recorded value
observed_at	Required	Time the value was observed
valid_from	Conditional	Start of validity period
valid_to	Conditional	End of validity period
reporting_period	Recommended	Reporting period
observation_quality	Recommended	Quality assessment
confidence	Optional	Confidence information
provenance_reference	Recommended	Origin and lineage
evidence_reference	Recommended	Supporting evidence
observation_status	Required	Validity or supersession status

The value representation must support the measurement types established by CR-MET-001.

A quantitative observation must be typed numerically. An ordinal observation must reference a valid ordinal scale. A categorical observation must reference a controlled vocabulary where appropriate.

6. Required relationships

Source	Relationship	Target
Measurement	applies Metric	Metric
Measurement	measures Subject	Any eligible enterprise entity
Measurement	produces	Observation
Observation	represents Metric	Metric
Observation	belongs to Measurement	Measurement
Observation	has provenance	Provenance
Observation	supported by	Evidence
Observation	references period	Reporting Period, where modeled

Cardinality requirements

* One Metric may be associated with zero or many Measurements.
* One Metric may be associated with zero or many Observations.
* One Measurement must reference exactly one Metric definition.
* One Measurement must reference one measurement subject.
* One Measurement may produce one or many Observations.
* One Observation must belong to exactly one Measurement.
* An Observation may have multiple supporting evidence references.

A Measurement should not be forced to produce an Observation if it fails, is cancelled or is otherwise incomplete.

7. Example

Consider Customer Retention Rate.

Metric definition

Customer Retention Rate

A reusable definition specifying the retention formula, customer population, period and unit.

Measurement

Customer retention measurement for Q3 2026, covering a specified customer population.

Observation

92.4%

Illustrative result, not actual enterprise data.

Evidence

Customer records, reporting-period boundaries, calculation output and source-system lineage.

This example demonstrates why a Metric must not be equated with its observed value.

8. Data integrity and validation

The implementation must validate:

* Measurement-to-Metric referential integrity.
* Subject identity and eligibility.
* Metric definition version consistency.
* Observation value type compatibility.
* Unit compatibility.
* Reporting-period consistency.
* Observation uniqueness where the measurement method requires it.
* Provenance requirements.
* Evidence references.
* Temporal validity.
* Supersession and correction semantics.

The CR must also investigate whether Observation should be a standalone registry entity, a runtime data entity or a hybrid metamodel construct.

My recommendation is to establish its canonical semantics in the metamodel while leaving high-volume operational observation storage to the future runtime architecture.

9. Implementation tasks

* Define Measurement semantics.
* Define Observation semantics.
* Add both entities to the normative metamodel.
* Establish identity and metadata requirements.
* Establish relationships and cardinalities.
* Define typed value representation.
* Define Metric version reference behavior.
* Establish provenance and evidence references.
* Implement validation schemas.
* Add representative Measurement and Observation fixtures.
* Update generated registries.
* Document runtime versus metamodel responsibilities.

10. Definition of Done

* Measurement and Observation formally defined.
* Both entities added to the normative metamodel.
* Identity and lifecycle requirements established.
* Relationships and cardinalities validated.
* Multiple measurement and observation scenarios supported.
* Numeric and non-numeric values supported.
* Metric version traceability established.
* Provenance and evidence relationships established.
* Failed and incomplete measurements supported.
* Registry and CI validation updated.
* Operational storage responsibilities documented.

CR-MET-004 — Establish Measurement Lifecycle and Execution Semantics

CR-MET-004

Execution lifecycle

Title: Establish Measurement Lifecycle, Execution States and Processing Semantics

Target repository: dea-metamodel, with runtime implementation implications

Priority: High

Depends on: CR-MET-001 and CR-MET-003.

1. Background and problem statement

Establishing Measurement and Observation entities is insufficient without defining how measurements are performed and how their lifecycle is managed.

Measurements may be:

* Automatically collected from enterprise systems.
* Calculated from multiple data sources.
* Recorded manually.
* Supplied by external providers.
* Derived from assessments.
* Generated through simulations.
* Produced by AI-enabled analytical agents.

Each method introduces different execution, validation and governance requirements.

OpenDEAM must establish a consistent lifecycle without unnecessarily prescribing one runtime implementation.

2. Objective

Define the canonical lifecycle and execution semantics governing the application of Metric definitions and the production of Observations.

The architecture must support automated, manual, externally sourced and computational measurement.

3. Proposed measurement lifecycle

flowchart TD
    A["Measurement Initiated"] --> B["Configuration Resolved"]
    B --> C["Source / Evidence Acquired"]
    C --> D["Measurement Performed"]
    D --> E["Calculation / Determination"]
    E --> F["Validation"]
    F --> G{"Valid?"}
    G -->|Yes| H["Observation Recorded"]
    H --> I["Observation Published"]
    G -->|No| J["Exception / Rejection"]
    J --> K["Correction or Retry"]
    K --> C
    I --> L["Measurement Completed"]
    L --> M["Audit and Provenance Retained"]

This is a logical lifecycle. Implementation may combine or split stages.

4. Measurement execution modes

The metamodel and associated documentation must recognize the following execution modes:

Mode	Description
Automated	Measurement initiated and executed by a system
Manual	Measurement recorded by an authorized person
Calculated	Result derived through a defined formula
Aggregated	Result derived from multiple observations
External	Measurement supplied by an external source
Assessed	Measurement derived from an assessment activity
Simulated	Measurement generated through a simulation
AI-assisted	Measurement determination supported by an AI process

These are execution classifications, not necessarily separate Measurement subclasses.

AI-assisted measurement must preserve the distinction between an AI-generated estimate and an independently verified observation.

5. Lifecycle states

Proposed canonical Measurement states:

State	Meaning
planned	Measurement scheduled or expected
initiated	Measurement process started
in_progress	Measurement being performed
pending_validation	Measurement result awaiting validation
validated	Measurement result passed validation
completed	Measurement successfully concluded
failed	Measurement execution failed
cancelled	Measurement cancelled
rejected	Result rejected through validation
superseded	Measurement replaced by a later or corrected record

The implementation must distinguish execution state from observation validity and publication state.

For example, a completed Measurement may produce an Observation that is subsequently corrected or superseded.

6. Processing semantics

The CR must establish:

A. Initiation

* Trigger conditions.
* Scheduled and event-driven execution.
* Manual initiation.
* Authorized initiators.
* Measurement context resolution.

B. Source acquisition

* Source identification.
* Source authorization.
* Data extraction.

* Source reliability.
* Source availability.

C. Determination

* Application of the metric formula.
* Unit conversion.
* Data transformation.
* Aggregation.
* Handling of missing values.
* Calculation reproducibility.

D. Validation

* Data type validation.
* Unit validation.
* Formula validation.
* Completeness.
* Plausibility.

E. Publication

* Observation recording.
* Result availability.
* Version association.
* Publication authorization.
* Notification or downstream integration.

F. Exception handling

* Failure classification.
* Retry policy.
* Manual intervention.
* Reconciliation.
* Correction.

7. Operational and governance requirements

The CR must establish:

CR-MET-005 — Establish Measurement Units, Formulas and Aggregation Semantics

CR-MET-005

Measurement semantics

Title: Establish Measurement Units, Dimensionality, Formula and Aggregation Semantics

Target repository: dea-metamodel

Priority: Critical

Depends on: CR-MET-001 and CR-MET-003; coordinated with CR-MET-004.

1. Background and problem statement

Metric definitions are only meaningful if their results can be interpreted consistently.

A value of 100 could represent:

* 100 employees.
* 100 customers.
* 100 milliseconds.
* 100 percent.
* An index score of 100.
* A currency amount of 100.

Even when two metrics use the same unit, their calculation methods may differ.

For example, an average processing time may be calculated as a simple mean or a weighted mean.

Likewise, summing percentages or averaging ratios can produce mathematically misleading results.

OpenDEAM therefore requires formal measurement semantics covering units, dimensions, formulas, precision and aggregation.

2. Objective

Establish a reusable measurement semantics architecture that allows OpenDEAM to:

* Represent measurement units and scales.
* Validate compatible measurement values.
* Define reproducible calculation methods.
* Support derived and composite metrics.
* Support mathematical aggregation.
* Preserve precision and rounding rules.
* Distinguish comparable from non-comparable results.
* Support interoperability with established unit systems.

3. Measurement unit architecture

The CR must distinguish:

Measurement Unit: A standardized unit in which a quantitative observation is expressed.

Measurement Scale: A system defining the meaning and permissible interpretation of values, including ordinal and categorical scales.

Examples:

Unit or scale	Measurement
Percentage (%)	Retention rate
Currency	Revenue
Count	Number of incidents
Time	Processing duration
Rate	Transactions per second
Ratio	Cost-to-income ratio
Ordinal scale	Maturity level
Categorical scale	Risk classification
Boolean scale	Pass/fail

Requirements

* Units must have stable identifiers.
* Units must have canonical symbols and definitions.
* Units must identify their measurement dimensions.
* Compatible unit conversion must be supported.
* Incompatible unit conversion must be rejected.
* Unit definitions must be reusable across Metrics.
* Units must not be duplicated inside individual Metric definitions.
* Ordinal and categorical scales must not be treated as physical measurement units.

The implementation should investigate alignment with UCUM and other established unit vocabularies rather than inventing a proprietary unit system.

4. Dimensionality

The metamodel should support measurement dimensions such as:

* Time.
* Length.
* Mass.
* Currency.
* Count.
* Temperature.
* Information quantity.
* Rate.
* Ratio.
* Dimensionless index.

Composite dimensions must be supported.

For example:

\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}

And:

\text{Throughput} =
\frac{\text{Completed Transactions}}{\text{Time}}

The architecture must distinguish dimensional compatibility from simple matching unit symbols.

5. Formula and calculation architecture

A Metric may use:

* Direct observation.
* Simple arithmetic calculation.
* Ratio or percentage calculation.
* Statistical calculation.
* Aggregation.
* Weighted calculation.
* Composite calculation.
* Derived calculation using other Metrics.

The CR should establish a canonical calculation-definition structure.

Element	Purpose
formula_id	Identifies calculation definition
expression	Defines calculation expression
inputs	Identifies required inputs
operators	Defines permitted operations
parameters	Defines configurable parameters
unit_semantics	Defines input and output units
precision	Defines computational precision
rounding_rule	Defines rounding behavior
version	Identifies formula version
validation_rules	Defines calculation constraints

These are logical fields. The actual implementation should reuse existing OpenDEAM expression or schema conventions where available.

Formula examples

Customer Retention Rate

CRR = \frac{E-N}{S}\times100

Where:

* = customers at end of period.
* = new customers acquired during period.
* = customers at start of period.

Mean Time to Recovery

MTTR = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} RecoveryTime_i}{n}

Capacity Utilization

CU = \frac{ActualCapacityUsed}{AvailableCapacity}\times100

The CR must require formula documentation, input definitions, calculation assumptions and handling of missing or invalid inputs.

6. Aggregation semantics

This is one of the most important components of the CR.

OpenDEAM must distinguish aggregation operations from the Metric definitions themselves.

Supported aggregation operators should include:

Operator	Purpose
SUM	Additive aggregation
COUNT	Count observations or entities
MIN	Minimum value
MAX	Maximum value
MEAN	Arithmetic average
WEIGHTED_MEAN	Weighted average
MEDIAN	Median
PERCENTILE	Distribution analysis
RATE	Quantity over time or another denominator
RATIO	Relationship between quantities
DISTINCT_COUNT	Count unique subjects

The metamodel must also establish aggregation compatibility rules.

For example:

* Counts may be additive when populations are mutually exclusive.
* Percentages should not ordinarily be summed.
* Ratios should not automatically be averaged.
* Weighted means require an explicitly defined weighting basis.
* Composite metrics require defined component weights and normalization.
* Ordinal values require scale-aware aggregation rules.

Example: Enterprise customer retention

Suppose two business units report:

Unit	Retained	Eligible
A	900	1,000
B	90	100

The enterprise retention rate is:

\frac{900+90}{1000+100}\times100=90\%

A simple average of the two rates also happens to be 90% here, but that is not generally true.

The calculation must therefore preserve numerator and denominator semantics where relevant.

7. Composite and derived Metrics

CR-MET-005 must establish the foundational calculation semantics for composite and derived Metrics, while leaving broader analytics and advanced interpretation to subsequent CRs.

Examples include:

* Composite Customer Experience Index.
* Enterprise Digital Maturity Index.
* Operational Resilience Index.
* Workforce Engagement Index.
* Enterprise Risk Exposure Index.

Requirements:

* Component Metric references.
* Component weights.
* Normalization method.
* Component contribution.
* Missing-component treatment.
* Aggregation method.
* Formula version.
* Output unit or scale.

9. Programme-wide acceptance conditions

There are five particularly important conditions I want preserved throughout implementation.

A. No semantic duplication

Do not create another Metric, Measurement, Classification or Identity construct if an existing OpenDEAM concept already provides the necessary semantics.

B. No unnecessary entity proliferation

Not every attribute or classification needs to become an entity. Introduce first-class entities where independent identity, lifecycle, governance, reuse or relationships justify them.

C. Preserve framework independence

Metric must not become a DMM-specific, KPI-specific or performance-management-specific construct.

D. Preserve operational separation

The metamodel defines the semantics. The runtime executes measurements and manages operational observations. The catalog provides reusable definitions.

E. Maintain backward compatibility

Existing Outcome, Scenario, Assessment and other downstream constructs must continue to function, or receive explicit and tested migration instructions.
